# Annotation Guidelines

This document describes the procedure used to construct the ground-truth attack
graphs in `dataset/log4j/` and `dataset/kaseya/`. It records the decision rules
the annotators followed, the evidence they were required to produce, and how
disagreements were resolved.

The output of this procedure is `ground_truth_edges.csv`, in which rows marked
`final_decision = ACCEPT` constitute the ground truth used by all evaluation
scripts, and `evidence_mapping.csv`, which records the supporting evidence
behind every decision.

---

## 1. Unit of annotation

The unit is a **candidate attack link**: a directed pair of CVEs together with a
proposed relationship type.

For each candidate link an annotator issued a single `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`
covering three questions at once:

1. **Existence** — does an attack relationship hold between these two
   vulnerabilities?
2. **Direction** — does it hold in the stated direction?
3. **Type** — is the proposed relationship type correct?

A candidate was accepted only if all three were satisfied. If any one failed,
the annotator issued `REJECT`. This is why agreement is reported per candidate
link rather than separately per question.

A CVE pair may appear more than once under different relationship types, and
each occurrence is judged on its own evidence. The two Log4j cases are
`CVE-2021-45046 → CVE-2021-45105`, rejected as `precondition_met` and accepted
as `incomplete_fix`, and `CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-44832`, rejected as
`incomplete_fix` and accepted as `similar_attack_pattern`. Rows in
`ground_truth_edges.csv` and `evidence_mapping.csv` are therefore keyed on
`(source_cve, target_cve, relationship_type)`, not on the CVE pair alone.

Every example in this document names the relationship type it was judged under,
because the same pair can carry different outcomes under different types.

## 3. Candidate link identification

Candidate links were not generated by enumerating all ordered CVE pairs. An
exhaustive enumeration would produce 156 pairs for Log4j and 702 for Kaseya,
the overwhelming majority of which have no documentary basis whatsoever.

Instead, candidates were identified by a **description-centered review** of the
CVE records and associated technical documentation. For each vulnerability the
reviewers examined:

- the exploitation mechanism,
- the affected product and version range,
- the exploitation preconditions,
- the post-exploitation impact,
- the remediation history,
- cross-references to other vulnerabilities.

A pair was promoted to a candidate link only when this review surfaced a
specific documentary basis for one of the four relationship types — a technical
dependency, an incomplete fix, a shared exploitation mechanism, or a
reconnaissance role. Pairs with no such basis were never entered into
`ground_truth_edges.csv`.

This yielded 16 candidate links for Log4j and 17 for Kaseya.

The consequence for interpreting the ground truth is that a link absent from
`ground_truth_edges.csv` was not judged and rejected; it was never proposed. The
`REJECT` rows are therefore the informative negatives: they record relationships
that looked plausible enough to reach adjudication and were nonetheless found
unsupported.

## 4. Evidence requirements

Every decision had to cite a source. `evidence_source` and
`evidence_excerpt_location` record which document was consulted and where in it
the relevant text appears; `excerpt_summary` in `evidence_mapping.csv` states
what the evidence establishes, in the annotators' own words.

Evidence was ranked in two tiers.

| Tier | Log4j | Kaseya |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | NVD records for the included CVEs; Apache Logging Services security advisories | NVD records; official Kaseya Unitrends security advisory of 3 December 2021 |
| Secondary | Peer-reviewed incident analyses and published technical reports | Public incident reports and related technical vulnerability analyses |

**A link could be accepted only on primary evidence.** Existence, direction, and
type each had to be either explicitly stated in a primary source or established
as a necessary technical dependency. Secondary sources could corroborate,
disambiguate, or provide context, but could not by themselves carry an
`ACCEPT`. Where vendor advisories and NVD both applied, the vendor advisory took
precedence.

---

## 5. Material consulted

Annotators worked from the CVE description together with the structured
attributes in `ground_truth_nodes.csv` — including `precondition` and
`postcondition` — and from the primary and secondary documents listed above.

The structured attributes served as an organizing aid: they made the
postcondition-to-precondition chain across a candidate pair easy to inspect and
state. They were **not** treated as evidence. Because `precondition`,
`postcondition`, and `vulnerability_type` are produced by the model under
evaluation, no candidate link was accepted on their basis. Every accepted link
was confirmed against the CVE description and the primary documentation, and any
candidate whose structured attributes suggested a relationship that the primary
sources did not support was rejected.

Section 12 records the residual limitation this arrangement leaves.

## 6. Relationship type criteria

### `incomplete_fix`

The target vulnerability exists because the remediation for the source
vulnerability was incomplete.

**Accept** when a primary source states that the fix for the source did not
fully address the underlying condition, or when the advisories together
establish that the released fix left the same exploitation path reachable.

**Reject** when the two vulnerabilities are merely sequential in time, affect
the same product, or were disclosed in the same remediation cycle, without any
statement that one arose from an incomplete fix of the other.

| | Candidate, judged as `incomplete_fix` | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | `CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-45046` | The NVD record and the Apache advisory both state explicitly that the Log4j 2.15.0 fix for CVE-2021-44228 was incomplete under certain non-default Pattern Layout and MDC configurations. |
| Accept | `CVE-2021-45046 → CVE-2021-45105` | Log4j 2.16.0 is the documented fix for CVE-2021-45046, yet the CVE-2021-45105 records state that versions through 2.16.0 remained vulnerable to uncontrolled recursion in the same lookup-processing path, requiring 2.17.0. |
| Reject | `CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-45105` | CVE-2021-45105 is a distinct recursion-based denial-of-service issue and does not reference CVE-2021-44228. The documented incomplete fix of CVE-2021-44228 is CVE-2021-45046. |
| Reject | `CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-44832` | CVE-2021-44832 exploits a separate JDBC Appender vector requiring attacker-controlled configuration. No advisory describes it as arising from an incomplete fix. |

The pair `CVE-2021-45046 → CVE-2021-45105` was proposed under two relationship
types and received opposite decisions. It is accepted here as `incomplete_fix`
on the strength of the remediation sequence, and rejected as `precondition_met`
in the next subsection because that judgment turns on different evidence. Both
rows are present in `ground_truth_edges.csv`, and the accepted one places the
pair in the ground truth.

### `precondition_met`

The postcondition of the source vulnerability satisfies the precondition of the
target vulnerability, so that exploiting the source places the attacker in a
position to exploit the target.

**Accept** when the state the attacker holds after exploiting the source is the
state the target requires, and both states are documented in primary sources.

**Reject** when the two conditions are merely compatible, when the required
state is a shared prerequisite of both vulnerabilities rather than a product of
exploiting the source, or when the privilege context differs.

| | Candidate, judged as `precondition_met` | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | `CVE-2021-43036 → CVE-2021-43038` | Weak wguest credentials yield wguest PostgreSQL account access, which is exactly the precondition CVE-2021-43038 requires. |
| Accept | `CVE-2021-43034 → CVE-2021-43043` | Code execution as the apache user is the access level CVE-2021-43043 requires for arbitrary file read. |
| Reject | `CVE-2021-45046 → CVE-2021-45105` | Control over Thread Context Map data is a shared prerequisite of both vulnerabilities, not something obtained by exploiting CVE-2021-45046. The pair enters the ground truth under `incomplete_fix` instead. |
| Reject | `CVE-2021-40387 → CVE-2021-43034` | CVE-2021-40387 yields code execution in the web server context; CVE-2021-43034 requires local user access on the appliance. The privilege contexts do not correspond. |

### `similar_attack_pattern`

Both vulnerabilities are exploited through the same underlying mechanism.

**Accept** when a primary source describes the two as operating in the same
fashion, or when both records independently describe the same mechanism —
the same component, the same class of flaw, and the same exploitation path.

**Reject** when the similarity is only at the level of outcome (both yield
code execution) or product, while the mechanism and entry point differ.

| | Candidate, judged as `similar_attack_pattern` | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | `CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-4104` | The CVE-2021-4104 description states directly that JMSAppender performs JNDI requests resulting in remote code execution in a similar fashion to CVE-2021-44228. |
| Accept | `CVE-2020-9493 → CVE-2022-23307` | The CVE-2022-23307 record states that this is the same Chainsaw deserialization issue previously identified as CVE-2020-9493, now in the component bundled with Log4j 1.2.x. |
| Accept | `CVE-2017-12477 → CVE-2021-43033` | Both records describe the bpserverd daemon reaching arbitrary code execution as root through input validation weaknesses. |
| Accept | `CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-44832` | Although the entry points differ, both records describe attacker-influenced JNDI resource resolution as the mechanism leading to remote code execution. The same pair is rejected as `incomplete_fix` above. |

### `reconnaissance`

The source vulnerability discloses information that materially assists
exploitation of the target.

**Accept** when the disclosed information is documented and bears on the target's
exploitation — configuration, credentials, service topology, or the shape of
attacker-reachable inputs.

**Reject** when the disclosure is unrelated to what the target requires.

| | Candidate, judged as `reconnaissance` | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | `CVE-2020-9488 → CVE-2021-44228` | SMTPS interception leaks log message content, revealing operational context and examples of processed log data useful for identifying the inputs targeted by CVE-2021-44228. |
| Reject | `CVE-2021-43039 → CVE-2021-43040` | Anonymous Samba read/write access does not disclose information bearing on the vaultServer writable-file flaw. |

## 7. Direction determination

Direction is decided per candidate link, and **the criterion differs by
relationship type**. The postcondition-to-precondition test applies to
`precondition_met` only; applying it to the other three types would be a
category error.

CVE numbering is never a criterion. Publication order is a criterion only for
`similar_attack_pattern`, and there only as a recording convention rather than a
claim about how an attacker proceeds.

### `precondition_met` — state dependency

Direction runs from the vulnerability whose postcondition supplies the required
state to the vulnerability whose precondition requires it. Both states were
confirmed against the CVE description and the official documentation before the
direction was fixed.

### `incomplete_fix` — remediation sequence

Direction runs from the vulnerability whose remediation was released first and
proved incomplete, to the follow-on vulnerability that the remediation failed to
close. The determining evidence is the advisory's fixed-version statement, not
the CVE identifier or the disclosure date. In `CVE-2021-45046 →
CVE-2021-45105`, for example, direction follows from 2.16.0 being the release
that fixed CVE-2021-45046 while leaving CVE-2021-45105 reachable.

### `reconnaissance` — information flow

Direction runs from the vulnerability that discloses information to the
vulnerability whose exploitation that information assists. Where a pair could be
argued in both directions, only the direction with documented informational
value was recorded.

### `similar_attack_pattern` — symmetric, with a recording convention

This relationship is semantically symmetric: two vulnerabilities that share an
exploitation mechanism share it in both directions, and neither is intrinsically
the source. It is nonetheless recorded once per pair in a fixed representative
direction, so that evaluation has a single target to match against and a
generated link in the opposite direction is scored deterministically.

**The representative direction places the earlier-disclosed vulnerability as
`source_cve`.** Disclosure date, not CVE number, is the criterion:
`CVE-2021-44228 → CVE-2021-4104` runs against numeric order because
CVE-2021-44228 was published first.

The consequence is that a method generating `CVE-2021-4104 → CVE-2021-44228` is
counted as a direction error even though the underlying claim is symmetric and
arguably correct. This is a scoring convention, not a substantive judgment, and
it applies uniformly to every evaluated method.

## 8. Disagreement resolution

Each annotator recorded a decision without visibility into the other's. Rows
where `annotator_1` and `annotator_2` differ were then re-examined against the
NVD record, the primary vendor advisory, and supporting technical reports, and
resolved by consensus into `final_decision`.

Two candidates required resolution:

| Dataset | Candidate | `annotator_1` | `annotator_2` | `final_decision` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Log4j | `CVE-2020-9488 → CVE-2021-44228` (`reconnaissance`) | ACCEPT | REJECT | ACCEPT |
| Kaseya | `CVE-2017-12477 → CVE-2017-12478` (`similar_attack_pattern`) | REJECT | ACCEPT | ACCEPT |

## 9. Second-pass review of out-of-reference links

Links produced by the evaluated methods that were absent from the initial
reference graph were not automatically counted as false positives. Every such
out-of-reference link was independently reviewed by the annotators under the
same evidence rules. A link was classified as hallucinated only when primary
evidence could establish neither an explicit nor a technically necessary
relationship.

This pass prevents the ground truth from penalizing correct relationships that
the initial description-centered review had simply not surfaced.

## 10. Agreement

Agreement was computed over all candidate links, treating each as a single
`ACCEPT`/`REJECT` judgment.

| Dataset | Candidate links | Agreement | Cohen's κ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apache Log4j | 16 | 93.8% | 0.88 |
| Kaseya Unitrends Backup | 17 | 94.1% | 0.85 |
| Overall | 33 | 93.9% | 0.86 |

The overall κ is the arithmetic mean of the two dataset values (0.862).
Computing κ over the pooled 33 judgments instead gives 0.869, because the
marginal distributions differ between the two datasets. The reported value uses
the mean so that neither dataset dominates.

Every figure in the table can be recomputed from `ground_truth_edges.csv`. The
script below covers both datasets and prints the pooled and mean values as well:

```python
import csv

def decisions(path):
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(open(path, encoding="utf-8-sig")))
    return ([r["annotator_1"].strip() for r in rows],
            [r["annotator_2"].strip() for r in rows])

def agreement(a1, a2):
    n = len(a1)
    po = sum(x == y for x, y in zip(a1, a2)) / n
    pe = sum((a1.count(c) / n) * (a2.count(c) / n) for c in set(a1) | set(a2))
    return n, po, (po - pe) / (1 - pe)

all_1, all_2, kappas = [], [], []
for case in ("log4j", "kaseya"):
    a1, a2 = decisions(f"dataset/{case}/ground_truth_edges.csv")
    all_1 += a1
    all_2 += a2
    n, po, k = agreement(a1, a2)
    kappas.append(k)
    print(f"{case:8s}  n={n:2d}  agreement={po:.3f}  kappa={k:.3f}")

n, po, k = agreement(all_1, all_2)
print(f"{'pooled':8s}  n={n:2d}  agreement={po:.3f}  kappa={k:.3f}")
print(f"{'mean':8s}  n={n:2d}  agreement={po:.3f}  "
      f"kappa={sum(kappas) / len(kappas):.3f}")
```

```
log4j     n=16  agreement=0.938  kappa=0.875
kaseya    n=17  agreement=0.941  kappa=0.850
pooled    n=33  agreement=0.939  kappa=0.869
mean      n=33  agreement=0.939  kappa=0.862
```

Comparing the two tables shows where adjudication was strictest.
`incomplete_fix` was proposed six times in Log4j and accepted twice: the
remaining four pairs were sequential releases of the same library with no
advisory describing one as an incomplete fix of the other. `precondition_met`
was rejected in all three Log4j cases and in two of eight Kaseya cases.
`similar_attack_pattern` was accepted every time it was proposed, reflecting
that a shared exploitation mechanism is usually stated outright in the records
rather than inferred.

The two cases exercise different parts of the taxonomy. Log4j relationships are
mostly shared-mechanism links within a single library, with a short
patch-evolution chain through CVE-2021-44228, CVE-2021-45046, and
CVE-2021-45105. Kaseya splits between shared-mechanism links and
privilege-chaining across appliance components, and contains no accepted
`incomplete_fix` or `reconnaissance` relationship at all.

## 12. Known limitations

**Structured attributes in the review material.** Annotators saw the
model-derived `precondition` and `postcondition` fields alongside the CVE
descriptions. Acceptance always required confirmation from a primary source, and
the structured fields were never sufficient on their own, but they did shape
which relationships were easiest to notice and articulate. Whether a fully blind
protocol would have produced a different candidate set is untested.

**Candidate set coverage.** Because candidates were surfaced by description-
centered review rather than exhaustive enumeration, a relationship that is
technically real but documented nowhere in the consulted sources would not have
entered adjudication. Recall figures should be read as recall against documented
relationships.

**Direction convention for symmetric relationships.** Recording
`similar_attack_pattern` in a single representative direction makes scoring
deterministic, but penalizes methods that generate the semantically equivalent
reverse link. The convention is applied uniformly, so comparisons between
methods remain fair, though absolute direction-error counts are inflated by it.

**Two annotators, two cases.** Agreement is high, but two annotators cannot
establish that the criteria generalize to other reviewers, and two case studies
cannot establish that they generalize to other vulnerability ecosystems.

**Documentation dependence.** The evidence rules deliberately exclude
relationships that the vendor and NVD do not describe. Where a technical
dependency exists but was never written down, the ground truth does not contain
it, and a method that infers it correctly is penalized.
